# Heart Disease Prevention Guidelines

Authorized medical bodies like the American Heart Association (AHA) and CDC recommend these lifestyle changes to reduce cardiovascular risk:

- **Regular Exercise**: Engage in at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity (such as brisk walking) or 75 minutes of vigorous activity weekly.
- **Healthy Eating Pattern**: Emphasize vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, healthy proteins (fish, poultry, nuts), and liquid non-tropical vegetable oils. Limit intake of saturated fats, sodium, processed meats, and sugar-sweetened beverages.
- **Smoking Cessation**: Quitting smoking is the single most effective action to improve cardiovascular health. Within years of quitting, heart disease risk drops to near that of a non-smoker.
- **Manage Blood Pressure and Cholesterol**: Track your lipid profiles and blood pressure. Work with a physician to maintain blood pressure < 120/80 mm Hg and LDL cholesterol within target ranges.
- **Control Fasting Blood Sugar**: Maintain a healthy weight and diet to prevent or manage type 2 diabetes, which strongly increases cardiovascular damage.
- **Regular Screening**: Schedule regular health checkups. A healthcare professional can perform comprehensive screenings to evaluate asymptomatic risks.
