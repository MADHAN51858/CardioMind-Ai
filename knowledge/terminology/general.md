# Cardiovascular Terminology

- **Systolic Blood Pressure**: The top number in a blood pressure reading. It measures the force your heart exerts on the walls of your arteries each time it beats. Normal systolic pressure is typically below 120 mm Hg.
- **Diastolic Blood Pressure**: The bottom number in a blood pressure reading. It measures the force your heart exerts on the walls of your arteries in between beats, while the heart is relaxed. Normal diastolic pressure is typically below 80 mm Hg.
- **Serum Cholesterol**: A waxy, fat-like substance found in your blood. High cholesterol can build up in your arteries, forming plaques that narrow the arteries and limit blood flow (atherosclerosis).
  - **HDL (High-Density Lipoprotein)**: Often called "good" cholesterol because it helps remove other forms of cholesterol from your bloodstream.
  - **LDL (Low-Density Lipoprotein)**: Often called "bad" cholesterol because high levels can lead to plaque buildup in your arteries.
- **Electrocardiogram (ECG / EKG)**: A simple test that records the electrical activity of your heart. It shows how fast your heart is beating, whether its rhythm is steady or irregular, and the strength and timing of electrical signals.
- **Angina**: Chest pain or discomfort caused when your heart muscle doesn't get enough oxygen-rich blood. It may feel like pressure or squeezing in your chest.
  - **Exercise-induced Angina**: Angina that is triggered by physical exertion or stress, when the heart requires more oxygenated blood than the narrowed coronary arteries can deliver.
- **ST Depression**: A finding on an ECG where the ST segment is abnormally low. In clinical stress tests, exercise-induced ST depression relative to rest (measured by 'oldpeak') is a strong indicator of myocardial ischemia (lack of blood flow to the heart muscle).
- **Thalassemia**: An inherited blood disorder that causes your body to have less hemoglobin than normal. Thalassemia-related factors in stress tests (thal) indicate heart perfusion defects (normal, fixed defect, or reversible defect).
