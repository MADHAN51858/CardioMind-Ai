# Cardiovascular Risk Factors

Heart disease risk is influenced by multiple factors, categorized into modifiable and non-modifiable factors:

### Modifiable Risk Factors
- **High Blood Pressure (Hypertension)**: Forces the heart to work harder, weakening it over time and damaging blood vessels.
- **High Blood Cholesterol**: Excess LDL cholesterol forms plaques in coronary arteries, restricting blood flow.
- **Fasting Blood Sugar (Diabetes)**: High blood glucose damages nerves and blood vessels over time, increasing the risk of heart disease.
- **Physical Inactivity**: Lack of exercise contributes to obesity, diabetes, and high blood pressure, increasing cardiac risk.
- **Obesity and Diet**: Diets high in saturated fats, trans fats, sodium, and refined sugars lead to lipid disorders and vessel blockages.
- **Smoking**: Carbon monoxide and nicotine in tobacco smoke damage cardiovascular lining, accelerate plaque deposition, and trigger spasms.
- **Stress and Sleep**: Chronic stress and poor sleep patterns are associated with elevated cortisol levels, higher blood pressure, and inflammation.

### Non-Modifiable Risk Factors
- **Age**: Risk of coronary artery narrowing increases naturally as blood vessels lose elasticity with age.
- **Sex**: Historically, males show a higher risk of heart disease at earlier ages, though females' risk increases significantly after menopause.
- **Family History**: Having a first-degree relative with early-onset heart disease indicates a genetic predisposition.
